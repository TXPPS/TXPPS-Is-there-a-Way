## The premise, as approved

An independent structural auditor is certifying a decommissioned mid-century
lock-and-dam on the Mississippi for demolition. Below the powerhouse is a
civil-defense shelter that was repurposed in the 1960s–70s for a federally
funded sensory-deprivation and isolation program.

An automated flood-response sequence seals every bulkhead on a river-gauge
reading that is physically impossible. Escape requires restoring three-phase
power, re-pressurising the gate hydraulics, and reaching the tainter gate
control house.

The person who sealed the player in is still down there, and believes he is
doing a kindness.

The title is the last audible line on the final program tape.

### Why it holds up

1. **The protagonist has a professional reason to be in every room.** An auditor
   certifying a complex for demolition is *supposed* to open panels, read
   gauges, and go below grade.
2. **The antagonist's method is the environment.** A spoofed gauge driving a real
   flood-response sequence is a lock a competent person can reason about, so the
   puzzles are diegetic and mechanical rather than bolted on.
3. **The title is diegetic.** It reframes the game on the last document found.
   That is an ending, not a twist.

## The three P4 sharpenings (approved 2026-08-27)

These are obligations on the P4 bible. None of them may be deferred to P6.

### S1 — Kindness must accrete, not land

The antagonist's belief that he is being kind has to be **legible early, in the
state of the rooms**, long before any tape says so:

- food staged for exactly one person, rotated, not hoarded
- a bunk kept made; a second bunk stripped
- hazards *fixed* rather than ignored — a taped cable, a barricaded drop, a
  hand-lettered warning in the right place
- a route that is survivable if you follow it and lethal if you do not

If his kindness only lands in the final tape, it is a reveal. If it accretes, it
is dread — and the player re-reads every earlier room the moment they understand.

**Test for P4:** a player who quits at the 40% mark should already be uneasy
about who has been tidying up.

### S2 — The programme needs a named funding line and a checkable purpose

Not "1970s experiments". P4 must name:

- the funding line (agency, programme designation, appropriation years)
- what it was actually measuring
- what equipment that measurement required
- what happened to the programme and why the facility kept the hardware

This is not set dressing. **The puzzles are built out of the equipment the
programme left behind**, so the equipment has to be the equipment that purpose
would actually require. A tank, a rack, a chart recorder and an intercom, chosen
because the programme needed them, will carry a puzzle. The same objects chosen
because they look creepy will not.

**Test for P4:** every puzzle device in Acts 2–4 traces back to a line in the
programme's own equipment schedule.

### S3 — The entity's rules derive from the programme's methodology

The player must be able to *learn* the rules, which means the rules must be
consistent, discoverable, and motivated. They come from what the facility did to
people: **what the programme did to its subjects is what the entity now does.**

That is the connective tissue between story and mechanic — it is why reading the
documents makes you better at surviving, rather than merely better informed.

The established visual motif (`ART_BIBLE.md`) is a vertical seam of light that
should not be there, and the established rule is that **the entity is only ever
between you and a light**. P4 must derive that rule from the methodology rather
than assert it: the programme's own procedure has to explain why.

**Test for P4:** the rule can be stated in one sentence by a player who has read
the documents, and that sentence is also a line in a programme document.
